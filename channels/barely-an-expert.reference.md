## Direct observations

History at 10 seconds shows a small-featured stick character studying a book, and at 353 seconds
a person at a desk with a thought bubble. Unexplored places at 570 seconds shows a perspective
cavern and at 755 seconds layered mountains. Science at 274 seconds uses a room, at 471 seconds
several reactions and at 942 seconds a simple labelled object comparison. These support varied
scenes with economical drawing. Dot/dash eyes and simple limbs are common, with exceptions for
garments or purposeful reactions. Keep the drawing economy and stable construction.

The five transcripts use ten-to-one countdowns, accessible connected prose, concrete details
and brief direct endings. Science uses explicit questions. Engineering develops mechanisms and
obstacles. History uses abstract categories. A myth vindication structure is not universal.
Do not invent universal disbelief, opposition or an unresolved mystery to fit a template.

## Writing and production contract

1. Read the source transcripts, then use a structure that fits the topic. The explicit default
   `source-grounded-explainer` avoids choosing a myth/failure/mystery template just because it
   was least recently used.
2. Research facts in primary sources: responsible institutions, datasets, original studies or
   historical collections. Keep a claim-to-source record with URL, relevant statement, date
   and uncertainty. Source-channel narration is style evidence, not scientific authority.
3. Separate measurements, estimates, hypotheses and unknowns. Date changing figures and check
   numerator, denominator and definition. Seafloor high-resolution mapping, satellite-inferred
   topography and direct camera observation are different measures.
4. Develop ten distinct chapters in about 2,500-2,650 words, using names and instruments where
   helpful. Vary sentences around a 13-16 word average; never apply a short sentence ceiling.
   Finish with a short original outro.
5. Plan people investigating, close-ups, settings, diagrams, comparisons and purposeful bubbles.
   A sentence may span image beats. Include framing, action and exact useful labels per prompt.
6. Compare varied generated samples and the thumbnail directly with actual source pixels before
   the full batch. Keep those source inputs present in later calls and after provider switches.
7. Assemble only after final voice and images. Inspect the resulting video. Compute chapters
   from final audio alignment and the actual timeline; rebuild after changed assets. File names,
   settings and reports alone do not verify that the final render contains the reviewed work.

Preserve the channel name, supplied topic title and existing voice identity. Study the broad
format and drawing vocabulary while creating original prose, dialogue and compositions.
