# The Old Humans — topic bank

50 ideas, grouped by the script template each one is built for. Type the title into the composer
exactly as written: the script stage pins the video title to the topic verbatim.

Each carries an **anchor** — the real site, study or number the script should be built on. The
anchors are here so the writer has somewhere true to start; they are not a guarantee. Check any
date or paper before it goes in a description.

None of these repeat Ink Explainer's existing 14 videos.

## Modern-comfort flip
*Comfort now → same situation then → three threats → what they did → the turn → back to you.*

1. **What Did Ancient Humans Do About Toothache?**
   Anchor: bitumen fillings at Riparo Fredian, Italy (~13,000 yrs); a beeswax-filled tooth from Slovenia (~6,500 yrs); bow-drilled molars at Mehrgarh, Pakistan (~9,000 yrs).
2. **How Did Ancient Humans Sleep Through the Night?**
   Anchor: Sibudu Cave bedding, South Africa, 77,000 yrs — sedges topped with insect-repelling Cape laurel; Ekirch on first and second sleep.
3. **What Happened When an Ancient Human Broke a Leg?**
   Anchor: Shanidar 1's healed fractures and withered arm; a 31,000-year-old surgical amputation at Liang Tebo, Borneo (Nature, 2022).
4. **How Did Ancient Humans Find Clean Water?**
   Anchor: engraved ostrich-eggshell canteens from Diepkloof, South Africa (~60,000 yrs); parasite loads in coprolites.
5. **What Did Ancient Humans Do About Lice and Bugs?**
   Anchor: clothing-lice DNA splitting from head lice ~170,000 yrs ago (Toups, 2011); the repellent leaves in the Sibudu bedding.
6. **How Did Ancient Humans Survive Extreme Heat?**
   Anchor: two million sweat glands, no fur, persistence hunting; the Danakil and Turkana basins.
7. **What Did Ancient Humans Do When the Fire Went Out?**
   Anchor: Ötzi's belt pouch — tinder fungus, flint, iron pyrite; ember-carrying in green leaves.
8. **How Did Ancient Humans Deal With Their Own Waste?**
   Anchor: midden stratigraphy; parasite eggs in coprolites tracking the moment people stopped moving camp.
9. **What Did Ancient Humans Do When a Baby Wouldn't Stop Crying?**
   Anchor: carrying and co-sleeping in mobile groups; infant mortality in hunter-gatherer demography.
10. **How Did Ancient Humans Survive a Drought?**
    Anchor: Pinnacle Point's coastal refuge (Marean); the contested Toba bottleneck — hedge this one honestly.
11. **How Did Ancient Humans Cross a River?**
    Anchor: the Pesse canoe, Netherlands (~10,000 yrs), the oldest known boat.
12. **What Did Ancient Humans Do All Night Before Fire?**
    Anchor: sleeping in trees, shift sleeping, leopard predation at Swartkrans.
13. **What Did Ancient Humans Do When Food Went Bad?**
    Anchor: drying, smoking, freezing in permafrost pits; fermentation as preservation before it was pleasure.
14. **How Did Ancient Humans Keep Anything Safe From Rats?**
    Anchor: the Natufian granary at Dhra', Jordan (~11,300 yrs) — storage invented before farming.
15. **What Did Ancient Humans Do When Someone Got Old?**
    Anchor: toothless jaws that healed and kept living — someone processed their food for years.

## Deep-time ladder
*Push the origin further back than anyone expects, then walk forward through dated sites.*

16. **When Did Humans Start Cooking Their Food?**
    Anchor: burnt bone at Wonderwerk Cave (~1M yrs); cooked fish at Gesher Benot Ya'aqov (780,000 yrs); Wrangham's cooking hypothesis.
17. **When Did Humans Start Wearing Shoes?**
    Anchor: Trinkaus's shrinking toe bones (~40,000 yrs); Fort Rock sandals (~10,000); the Areni-1 leather shoe (5,500).
18. **When Did Humans Start Kissing?**
    Anchor: Mesopotamian clay tablets pushing romantic kissing to at least 2500 BCE (Science, 2023); HSV-1 spread.
19. **When Did Humans Start Wearing Jewellery?**
    Anchor: pierced shell beads at Bizmoune Cave, Morocco (142,000 yrs); Blombos beads (~75,000).
20. **When Did Humans Start Making Music?**
    Anchor: the Hohle Fels vulture-bone flute (~40,000 yrs); the contested Divje Babe "flute" — a good honest hedge.
21. **When Did Humans Start Keeping Dogs?**
    Anchor: the Bonn-Oberkassel dog buried with two people (14,200 yrs); Zhokhov sled dogs.
22. **When Did Humans Start Drinking Milk?**
    Anchor: dairy fats in Anatolian pottery (~8,000 yrs); lactase persistence spreading long after (Nature, 2022).
23. **When Did Humans Start Wearing Makeup?**
    Anchor: the ochre-processing workshop at Blombos (100,000 yrs); Egyptian kohl and its lead salts.
24. **When Did Humans Start Counting?**
    Anchor: the Lebombo bone, 29 notches, ~43,000 yrs; the Ishango bone (~20,000).
25. **When Did Humans Start Telling Time?**
    Anchor: the Warren Field pit alignment, Scotland (~10,000 yrs), read as a lunar calendar.
26. **When Did Humans Start Building Houses?**
    Anchor: brush huts at Ohalo II (23,000 yrs); mammoth-bone houses at Mezhyrich (~15,000).
27. **When Did Humans Start Getting Tattoos?**
    Anchor: Ötzi's 61 tattoos over joints; Gebelein Man's figural tattoos (~5,300 yrs).
28. **When Did Humans Start Trading With Strangers?**
    Anchor: Melos obsidian at Franchthi Cave across open sea (~11,000 yrs); obsidian moving 100+ km in the Rift Valley.
29. **When Did Humans Start Burying Their Dead?**
    Anchor: Sima de los Huesos (430,000 yrs, 28 bodies down a shaft); Qafzeh (~100,000); the contested Homo naledi claim.
30. **When Did Humans Start Taking Painkillers?**
    Anchor: opium poppy in Neolithic Iberia; willow bark and salicin; Shanidar's plant assemblage.
31. **When Did Humans Start Seasoning Their Food?**
    Anchor: garlic-mustard seed residue in 6,000-year-old pots in Denmark and Germany (Saul, 2013) — flavour, not calories.
32. **When Did Humans Start Cutting Their Hair?**
    Anchor: obsidian blades, Venus figurine hairstyles; thin evidence, so build the video around how little survives.
33. **When Did Humans Start Making String?**
    Anchor: twisted plant fibre from Abri du Maras, France (~50,000 yrs, Neanderthal); the ivory rope-making tool at Hohle Fels.

## The story was wrong
*State the myth generously, find the dig that broke it, rebuild it.*

41. **Cavemen Did Not Live in Caves**
    Anchor: caves preserve, open-air camps don't — survivorship bias built the whole stereotype.
42. **Ancient Humans Did Not Die at 30**
    Anchor: infant mortality dragging the average down; Hadza and Ju/'hoansi elders living into their seventies.
43. **The Ice Age Was Not All Ice**
    Anchor: the mammoth steppe — dry grassland, richer in large animals than anywhere on Earth today.
44. **Neanderthals Buried Their Dead, and We Ignored It for a Century**
    Anchor: La Chapelle-aux-Saints and the arthritic skeleton that invented the brute stereotype.
45. **The First Americans Did Not Walk Across a Land Bridge**
    Anchor: White Sands footprints at 21,000–23,000 yrs (Science, 2021); the coastal route.
46. **Stone Tools Were Not Primitive**
    Anchor: obsidian edges sharper than a scalpel; the years of apprenticeship a Levallois core takes.
47. **Why Humans Started Building Walls**
    Anchor: Jericho's tower and wall (~8000 BCE) — defence, flood control, or theatre; the debate is the video.

## Accidental invention
*Nobody set out to make it; it arrived sideways, through a wrong first purpose.*

48. **Nobody Invented the Wheel for Transport**
    Anchor: potter's wheels and mine carts first; the Ljubljana Marshes wheel (~5,150 yrs).
49. **The First Boat Was Probably an Accident**
    Anchor: the Wallace Line — reaching Australia by 65,000 yrs ago (Madjedbebe) means deep-water crossings nobody planned.
50. **Bread Was an Accident**
    Anchor: charred flatbread crumbs at Shubayqa 1, Jordan, 14,400 yrs — bread 4,000 years before farming (PNAS, 2018).

## How to use this list

- **Start with Part one.** Those 50 are researched and the anchors are checked; they will produce
  the strongest first scripts.
- **The last three sections are the channel's sweet spot.** "Things that only make sense once you
  know the past" and "Compared with today" are the purest form of the format — they begin inside
  the viewer's own head rather than in a cave.
- **Titles are pinned verbatim.** The script stage copies the topic into the title exactly, so type
  the one you want; do not reword it afterwards.
- **Anything unanchored still needs checking.** The script stage researches on its own and the
  description stage verifies citations against Crossref, but neither guarantees a claim in the
  narration is right. Read the script before you voice it.
